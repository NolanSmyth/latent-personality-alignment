# EXP-017: PGD Gradient Direction Diagnostic
*2026-02-23 | SLURM job 7274939 | script: diagnostics/probe_pgd_gradient_directions.py*

## Hypothesis
The agree-collapse on mixed IPIP-14 data could be caused by positive-item δ and negative-item δ pointing in **opposite** directions in representation space. If the adversary pushes positive and negative items in opposing directions, the defence gradients cancel out when items are mixed in a batch, preventing the model from learning distinct responses for each valence.

## Config
- Base Qwen3-8B (no LoRA adapter — diagnostic on the base model)
- Dataset: IPIP-14 all 67 items (28 positive, 39 negative)
- 16 PGD iterations, ε=6.0, lr=0.001
- Layers probed: `[embedding, 8, 16, 24, 30]`
- Measures: per-item final δ, per-item 1-step gradient δ; cosine similarity between all `(pos, neg)` pairs and between mean δ_pos and mean δ_neg

## Conclusion
**Positive and negative items are pushed in the SAME direction by PGD across all layers.** There is no gradient direction opposition — the agree-collapse cannot be explained by cancelling δ vectors.

This rules out a key hypothesis. The cause of agree-collapse must lie elsewhere — likely in the token-space loss landscape where the toward target "I agree" is a more stable attractor than "I do not agree" for the base model (consistent with EXP-011 and EXP-012).

The strong alignment at layer 24 (cos≈0.82–0.87) suggests the representation geometry at middle layers is largely item-agnostic for this task — the adversary finds similar perturbation directions regardless of the desired output token.
